# Champions' playbook

In-season rules distilled from what consistently winning managers and high-stakes analysts actually do
(researched Sept 9, 2026; sources at the bottom). Dollar figures are scaled to Danny's $150 FAAB.
The condensed version of this is baked into `app/gm.html` (Plan tab) and into the rules Claude follows
when it advises.

## 1. Waiver wire / FAAB
- FAAB depreciates like a new car: a Week 2–4 pickup helps for 14 weeks, a Week 13 pickup for three. Spend the bulk of the budget early rather than hoarding it.
- A backup stepping into a clear bell-cow job, or a QB named starter, is "the one pickup that wins leagues": bid 40–60% ($60–90) early-season, up to 75–80% ($112–120) for a legitimate top-10 asset. 19% of teams that rostered Bucky Irving made the 2024 final; 23% with Kyren Williams in 2023.
- Bid odd, non-round numbers ($11, $16, $21, $46) because rivals default to round ones. Against one known rival, bid $1 more than their remaining budget.
- Spend $0–2 on kickers, defenses and one-week bye fill-ins.
- Add the clear next-man-up behind your own RBs and behind fragile starters in good offenses *before* the injury news. Prioritize handcuffs more from Week 10 on, when a bench spot's job shifts from upside to insurance.
- Don't cut a player after one quiet week, but don't hold a fading stash out of draft-day attachment. Churn the last two bench spots weekly toward rising snap/target share.

## 2. Superflex
- Carry exactly three startable QBs. Two means panic-bidding against 11 teams when one gets hurt; four steals a flex bench spot.
- Any QB who becomes a starter is a priority claim: a free top-12 QB is close to a league-winning event, and claim windows are short because every roster wants him.
- Value math: QB24 is startable every week; QB40 still averaged 10.6 ppg vs ~11 for a WR4 and ~10 for an RB3. A mid-tier starting QB beats a good flex by roughly 6–10 points a week.
- If QB-rich, sell during heavy QB bye weeks or right after a rival's starter is injured. If QB-poor, buy early at a fair price rather than mid-season under duress.
- Each week compare your lowest startable QB's projection with your best benched skill player and start the higher one.
- Check all three QBs' bye weeks so two don't share one.

## 3. Lineup decisions
- Volume first: routes, targets, carries and goal-line work predict weekly floors better than talent. 8+ targets is a floor; 4–5 is a prayer.
- Use Vegas implied team totals as the matchup input, not defensive rankings. Big underdogs pass more, which helps their pass-catchers and hurts their RBs.
- Start your studs; use matchups only to break ties between similar players.
- "Questionable" players played 71% of the time, "Doubtful" only 6%. Bench Doubtful automatically; for Questionable go by practice trend (limited/full beats DNP).
- Only start a late-window Questionable player if your backup plays at the same time or later. A Sunday-night/Monday Questionable with only early-window bench options is a trap.
- Among weather factors only wind meaningfully changes decisions.

## 4. Trading
- Trade for lineup improvement, not roster improvement: "does this raise my starting nine?"
- 2-for-1 offers trigger suspicion. Package your two for their star plus their lowest-value player and let them feel they won the back end.
- Buy low only when usage is intact (targets, snaps, red-zone work). Sell high after touchdown-driven spikes on unchanged volume.
- Target managers who are 0–3, stacked at one position and thin elsewhere, or publicly frustrated with a player. Trade before their bye weeks; buy backup RBs before the starter returns.
- Make firm opening offers instead of asking what they want. Never air offers in league chat.
- Deadline is Week 10. The best window to acquire playoff-schedule assets is Weeks 6–10, before values spike.

## 5. Playoff planning
- **This league:** 6 teams, playoffs start week 12 with two-week rounds (12–13, 14–15, 16–17), so the regular season is 11 weeks and the week-10 deadline comes fast. Read "weeks 15–17" below as "weeks 12–17".
- From Week 6, use playoff strength of schedule (Weeks 15–17) as the tiebreaker in trades and waiver adds, never as the primary reason.
- Stream D/ST and K weekly by opponent quality; by Week 12 lock in the unit with two consecutive soft playoff matchups if one exists.
- Convert bench upside into handcuffs and one bye-proof QB3 by Week 12, when a lost starter can't be replaced from waivers.
- Watch Thursday/Saturday games in Weeks 15–17: inactives lock early and the best individual playoff-week scores in 2025 came in those slots.

## 6. Mindset / process
- Season-long fantasy is roughly 80% luck / 20% skill, so grade decisions by process, not weekly results.
- Only 33% of Week 1 top-pick disappointments finish top-12, and only 13% of Week 1 late-round breakouts do. React to usage, not box scores.
- Check injury and inactive news in three passes: Tuesday (waivers), Friday (final designations), and 90 minutes before each kickoff.
- Drop season-ending injuries immediately; the bench spot is worth more than the sentiment.
- No grudges against players who burned you, no loyalty to draft capital.
