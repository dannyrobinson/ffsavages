The one idea

With two QB slots and 12 teams, 24 quarterbacks start every week and there are maybe 26 you'd actually want. An elite QB scores 23–25 a week; the QB you'd otherwise start in your superflex slot scores 13–14. That 10-point weekly gap is the biggest edge on the board, and it's bigger than Gibbs over a mid-RB2.

But this room was a 1QB league last year

Superflex is new to this league in 2026. Last year's draft (1QB, same 12 managers) went zero QBs in round 1, four in round 2 (Allen at 13, Lamar 15, Daniels 16, Hurts 24), then none until Burrow and Mahomes at 45–46. You won it at 11–3 with Kyler in round 7 and Tua in round 13. Rooms like this usually under-adjust to a new superflex slot: some managers will read one article and go QB early, most will draft the way they always have. That means QBs will probably go later here than industry ADP, and your second and third QB can come cheaper than the rankings say.

The three managers who took QBs early last year are picking from slots 12, 1 and 2 this year (TheBigHelmet, EasyIP, kwiksl). Expect picks 1–2 and 12–13 to be QB-heavy; the rest of the room is the wild card.

Read the room at 1.06

Look at picks 1–5 before you decide. If three or more are QBs, the room has adjusted — take the best tier-1 QB left (Maye, Burrow, Daniels, Lamar) and don't overthink it. If two or fewer are QBs, the room is drafting like 2025 — take Gibbs, Bijan or Chase, because they will not be there at 19, and a top-9 QB almost certainly will. Either way you leave round 3 with at least one QB1 and ideally two.

Rounds 2–3: get to two QBs

Your picks are 19 and 30. If you took a skill player at 6, take the best QB at 19 and another at 30 if a top-14 QB is there — two starting QBs by pick 30 puts you ahead of most of this room. If you took a QB at 6 and another tier-1 QB survived to 19, take him too; a WR1 will still be there at 30 in this room. What you should not do is leave round 3 with zero quarterbacks.

Tight end is worth more here

Half a point per catch on top of full PPR means Bowers, McBride, Loveland and Warren are effectively WR2s. If one falls to 43, that's a better pick than the fifth-best RB on the board. Don't reach for one at 30, though — QB or WR first.

Third QB

Take one around round 8–9 (Shough, Darnold, Ward, Daniel Jones, Stroud, Bryce Young). Bye weeks with two starting QBs will bite, and a spare QB is the best trade chip in a 2QB league by October.

No keepers

Confirmed with Danny: nobody keeps anyone, everyone drafts from scratch. McCaffrey is back in the pool — he's an RB1 on the board, but at 30 with his injury history he's a round-3/4 pick here, not a round-1 pick.
